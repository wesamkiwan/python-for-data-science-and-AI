# Capstone 1: Customer Churn — End-to-End EDA & ML Project

🟡 **Difficulty:** Intermediate | ⏱️ **Estimated Time:** 4-6h | **Unlocks after:** Module 15 (Ensemble Methods & Advanced ML)

## 🎯 What This Capstone Demonstrates

This project applies everything from **Phase 2 (Data Science Core)** and **Phase 3 (Machine Learning)** — Modules 06 through 15 — to one realistic, end-to-end business problem, exactly the shape of work you'd be handed in your first weeks at a real data science job.

---

## 📋 The Scenario

You're a data scientist at **StreamLine**, a mid-sized subscription streaming service. The VP of Customer Success has come to you with a request:

> "We're losing subscribers and don't fully understand why. Can you dig into our customer data, tell us what's actually driving cancellations, and build something that flags at-risk customers *before* they leave — so our retention team can reach out proactively?"

You've been handed a raw export from the billing and support systems: `customer_churn.csv`. It has **not** been cleaned — like most real data handed to you on the job, it has missing values, a few data-entry errors, inconsistent text formatting, and even some accidental duplicate rows from a double-import.

⚠️ **Note:** This dataset is synthetic (no real customers), generated by `generate_dataset.py` in this folder, but deliberately modeled on realistic subscription-churn patterns and messiness — everything about the cleaning and analysis process is authentic practice for real-world data.

## 📦 What You're Given

- `customer_churn.csv` — 2,008 customer records, 11 columns (already generated and included in this folder; re-run `generate_dataset.py` if you want to regenerate it or inspect exactly how it was built).
- **Columns:** `customer_id`, `age`, `tenure_months`, `contract_type`, `internet_service`, `tech_support`, `payment_method`, `monthly_charges`, `total_charges`, `num_support_calls`, `churn` (the target: `1` = churned, `0` = retained).

## ✅ Requirements

Your deliverable is a complete analysis and model that:

1. **Cleans the data** — handle missing values, duplicates, inconsistent text formatting, and outliers, with documented reasoning for each decision (Modules 08, 13a).
2. **Explores the data** — understand the churn rate, key distributions, and which factors correlate with churn, using appropriate visualizations and statistics (Modules 09, 10).
3. **Prepares features** — encode categorical variables and scale numeric ones correctly, using a leakage-safe pipeline (Modules 07, 13a, 13b).
4. **Compares at least 3 models** — including at least one linear model and at least one ensemble method — using proper cross-validation, not just a single train/test split (Modules 12, 13c, 15).
5. **Tunes the best-performing model's hyperparameters** (Module 15c).
6. **Evaluates honestly** — report accuracy, precision, recall, F1, and AUC; explicitly discuss the precision/recall tradeoff given this is an imbalanced classification problem (Module 12b).
7. **Interprets the model** — identify and explain the top factors driving churn in plain business language (Module 12c, 15a).
8. **Writes a business-facing summary** — a short, non-technical writeup of your findings and a concrete recommendation, following Module 10's "separate description from inference" discipline.

## 💡 How to Use This Capstone

1. Try the analysis yourself first, using `starter-guide.md` if you get stuck on what to do next (not how to do it).
2. Compare your approach against `solution.md` — there's no single "correct" answer in real data science, so differences in judgment calls (which outliers to remove, which model to pick) are normal and worth reflecting on.
3. Read `portfolio-presentation.md` once you're happy with your version, and adapt this project for your own portfolio/GitHub/interview talking points.
